\small\textbf{Findings in brief.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=0.1em]
\item The multiplier is cheap. An int8 dot product is paid for in instruction fetch and decode, operand delivery and loop control (Horowitz, 2014; Dally et al., 2020).
\item The remedy is the DSP recipe: a single-cycle MAC into a wide accumulator, two operand reads per cycle, post-incrementing address generators and zero-overhead loops. DSPs have had it since the 1980s; RISC-V and Arm microcontroller extensions have repeated it since 2017.
\item Bit-serial, compute-in-BRAM, dataflow and EDGE designs carry overheads that do not pay off for one neuron on an FPGA with 240 hard DSP slices.
\item The LS design adopts the hardware repeat as \texttt{MAC \#n}. It passes the review's three-part structural test, and the predicted 64-input neuron takes 112 cycles against 300 for the best conventional code on the same core.
\item No mechanism is claimed as new. What is uncommon is the combination at 8-bit scale and its honest measurement on the board.
\end{enumerate}
